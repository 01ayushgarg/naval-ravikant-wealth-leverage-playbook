# Worked example 2: a pre-seed founder deciding how to raise

> **Fictional.** Kofi, his co-founder, the company, the investors and every number below are invented to show how
> the skill works. Nothing here is a claim about any real person or company. Quotes from Naval Ravikant are real
> and cited. Not legal or investment advice.

## What he sent

```text
Run Naval's wealth and leverage playbook on my startup.

What we do: software that predicts no-shows for physiotherapy clinics and fills the slots.
How we're paid: $300/month per clinic. 60 clinics, $18,000 MRR, growing about 12% a month.
Team: me (product and code) and Ama (ex-clinic manager, does all sales). 50/50, no vesting yet.
Money: $400,000 left from friends and family; burn $45,000/month.
Decision: one fund offers $2M at a $20M post-money, with a board seat and a 2x participating
preference. Angels would do $1.5M at a $12M post-money on a standard SAFE. Also: we need a
first engineer and a recruiter wants 25% of first-year salary to find one.
```

## What the skill returned

### Where you are

Founders with real traction and a builder-seller pair: Kofi builds, Ama sells. That's the pair he describes as
usual for good founding teams [NAV-curate-people]. The weak spot is the co-founder paperwork: no vesting.

### Fundraising: timing and order

Runway is $400,000 ÷ $45,000, about nine months. He prefers starting a raise "preferably six to 12 months before we
need it." [NAV-sell] So start now. His order of what investors weigh is "traction then team then social proof then
product." [VH-no-lead] Kofi's strongest card is traction. → `16-fundraising-and-term-sheets.md`

### The two offers, through `templates/09-deal-and-fundraise-check.md`

| | Fund | Angels |
|---|---|---|
| Money | $2M | $1.5M |
| Dilution | 10% | 12.5% |
| Lock-in | Board seat, 2x participating preference | None beyond the SAFE |
| Exit | Hard: a bad investor on the board is hard to walk away from [NAV-sell] | Easy |
| Upside of the extra $500,000 | Six to eight more months of runway | n/a |

**Our arithmetic (invented):** on a $40M sale, a 2x participating preference on $2M takes $4M off the top, then a
share of the rest. That's money out of every common holder's pocket in a modest outcome, which is the likeliest
good outcome. The 2.5-point dilution difference is the small split; the preference and board seat are the long
terms. His view of deals: "You have to be able to walk away from deals." [NAV-sell] **Verdict (our reading):**
take the angel round, or counter the fund with no participation and no board seat. Walk away if refused.

### Who runs the raise, and the first hire

"You can't outsource fundraising because investors are betting on you." [NAV-curate-people] Kofi and Ama run it.
The same episode applies to the engineer: he argues founders can't outsource recruiting either
[NAV-curate-people]. **Our suggestion:** skip the recruiter; Kofi sources from open-source contributors to the
scheduling libraries they already use, and scores each with `templates/08-hiring-scorecard.md`. The first engineer
gets real equity, in the spirit of his "late founders" line [NAV-why-you-cant-hire].

### Co-founder vesting

He calls founder vesting the pre-nup [VH-pick-cofounder]. Both founders agree to four-year vesting with credit for
the year already worked, before any money arrives.

### AI leverage

Their onboarding is manual: Ama spends 10 hours a week importing clinic calendars. A coding agent builds an import
tool in a week, which frees a quarter of the company's selling time. → `17-ai-era-leverage-and-code.md`

## The session notes (excerpt)

**This month**
1. Sign founder vesting. [VH-pick-cofounder]
2. Start the angel raise; send a two-paragraph update with the traction numbers to 30 warm contacts. [NAV-sell]
3. Build the calendar-import tool; free Ama's 10 hours. [NAV-industrial]

**The number:** months of runway at the end of the raise. Today: 9. Target: 24.

**Where to be careful:** a 12% monthly growth rate rarely lasts. Plan the raise on a slower rate, and don't let a
"hot" term sheet with a short deadline rush the decision (wait 24 hours, chapter 10).

## The result (invented)

The angels closed $1.5M in seven weeks. The fund later offered the same price with no board seat; they took
$300,000 of it. The first engineer came from the open-source list and shipped the import tool in her second week.
