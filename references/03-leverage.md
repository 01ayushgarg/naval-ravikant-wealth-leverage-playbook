## Fortunes require it

The thread says fortunes require leverage, and that business leverage comes from capital, people, and products
with no marginal cost of replication: code and media
[TWEET18 #22](https://x.com/naval/status/1002106317064949763). The tweet before it is Archimedes on a long enough
lever [#21](https://x.com/naval/status/1002106224123432960). In 2020 he widened the list to include community
[NAV-least].

> "It doesn't take money to make money, it takes leverage to make money."
> [X 2022-03-13](https://x.com/naval/status/1503125171745411073)

## The four kinds, ranked

| Kind | Permission? | His verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Labor** | Someone has to follow you | "I would argue that this is the worst form of leverage that you could possibly use. Managing other people is incredibly messy." [RICH-21] |
| **Capital** | Someone has to give it to you | Scales well: managing more capital is easier than managing more people [RICH-21] |
| **Code** | None | "Coding is such a great superpower because now you can speak the language of the robot armies and you can tell them what to do." [RICH-22] |
| **Media** | None | He calls the podcast itself a form of leverage [RICH-22] |

On labor he says you want the minimum number of people that lets you use the other kinds [RICH-21]. The thread
adds that labor leverage will impress your parents but isn't worth chasing
([#24](https://x.com/naval/status/1002106698889183234)), and that to raise capital you apply specific knowledge
with accountability and show good judgement ([#23](https://x.com/naval/status/1002106623639212032)).

## Permissioned and permissionless

> "Code and media are permissionless leverage."
> [TWEET18 #26](https://x.com/naval/status/1002106893265920000)

Capital and labor are permissioned: someone has to give you money or follow you
([#25](https://x.com/naval/status/1002106775036874752)). Code and media work while you sleep
([#26](https://x.com/naval/status/1002106893265920000)). If you can't code, the thread says, write books and
blogs, or record videos and podcasts ([#28](https://x.com/naval/status/1002107377598873600)).

> "This newest form of leverage is where all the new fortunes are made, all the new billionaires." [RICH-22]

The idea predates the thread. In 2011 he posted: "Code is the ultimate force multiplier and its leverage advantage
is increasing." [X 2011-07-24](https://x.com/naval/status/95250260523368449)

## Pick a business model with built-in leverage [RICH-24]

| Property | His words |
|---|---|
| Scale economies | "You should try and get into a business where making Widget Number 12 is cheaper than making Widget Number 5" [RICH-24] |
| Zero marginal cost | The next copy of what you made is free [RICH-24] |
| Network effects | "A network effect is when each additional user adds value to the existing user base." [RICH-24] |

He also argues code and media are the more ethical routes, since the products are equally available to everyone
[RICH-23].

## The ladder: one industry, rising leverage [RICH-25]

His worked example is real estate. Our summary of the rungs he describes:

| Rung | Specific knowledge | Accountability | Leverage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Day laborer | Little; replaceable | "a faceless cog" [RICH-25] | Tools only |
| General contractor | Some | Takes the job's risk | Labor |
| Property developer | Which lots, which neighbourhoods | More risk | Labor and capital |
| Famous architect or large developer | Deep | A name that adds value | Labor, capital, brand |
| Real estate investment trust | Real estate plus capital markets | High | Capital |
| Real estate tech company | Real estate plus technology | Very high | Code, capital, the best labor |

## Leverage keeps rising

In 2009 he argued that the leverage available to an Internet entrepreneur was far greater than in earlier
generations: "No, people aren't getting any smarter or harder-working. But the amount of leverage is obscene."
[NAV-the-returns-to-entrepreneurship] In 2026, on programmers working with AI: "these are now among the most
leveraged people on earth." [NAV-ai] And: "Intelligence is not normally distributed. Leverage is not normally
distributed." [NAV-ai]

## How to apply it (our reading)

1. **Inventory.** For each kind (labor, capital, code, media), write what you have and hours a week on it.
2. **Start permissionless.** Pick one code or media asset you can ship this month with nobody's approval.
3. **Add a business-model property.** Ask which of scale economies, zero marginal cost or network effects your
   product could gain [RICH-24].
4. **Earn permissioned leverage last.** Use a public track record to attract capital and people
   ([#23](https://x.com/naval/status/1002106623639212032)).
5. **Find your rung** on your industry's ladder and name the next one [RICH-25].

**Worked number (invented).** A tax adviser bills 25 client hours a week at $150, about $15,000 a month, capped
by her calendar. Option A, labor: hire a junior at $4,000 a month who bills 25 hours at $90. Net gain about
$5,500 a month, plus management time. Option B, code and media: a $29-a-month tax-deadline checklist app for
freelancers, sold through her newsletter. At 300 subscribers that's $8,700 a month, and the 301st customer costs
her nearly nothing. A is faster and surer; B compounds and needs nobody's permission. Our reading of his ranking
says start B, and hire only when B creates work only a person can do.

**Failure modes (our reading):**
- Hiring to look bigger (labor as status, the thing #24 warns against).
- Building media with no specific knowledge behind it. Reach without substance doesn't convert.
- Raising capital before you have a record that justifies it.
- Code that needs you to run it every day is a job, not leverage.

**Limits:** zero marginal cost doesn't apply to physical goods or care work. There the ladder (labor, then
capital, then brand) is the more honest map. See chapter 18.
