## Fortunes require it

> "Fortunes require leverage. Business leverage comes from capital, people, and products with no marginal cost
> of replication (code and media)." [TWEET18 #22](https://x.com/naval/status/1002106317064949763)

The tweet before it is Archimedes on a lever long enough to move the earth [TWEET18 #21](https://x.com/naval/status/1002106224123432960).
In 2020 he widened the list: "You can be leveraged through code, community, media, capital, labor and other
ways." [NAV-least]

## The four kinds, ranked

| Kind | Permission? | His verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Labor** | Someone has to follow you | "I would argue that this is the worst form of leverage that you could possibly use. Managing other people is incredibly messy." [RICH-21] |
| **Capital** | Someone has to give it to you | "It scales very, very well. If you get good at managing capital, you can manage more and more capital much more easily than you can manage more and more people." [RICH-21] |
| **Code** | None | "Coding is such a great superpower because now you can speak the language of the robot armies and you can tell them what to do." [RICH-22] |
| **Media** | None | "This podcast is a form of leverage." [RICH-22] |

> "Labor means people working for you. It's the oldest and most fought-over form of leverage. Labor leverage will
> impress your parents, but don't waste your life chasing it."
> [TWEET18 #24](https://x.com/naval/status/1002106698889183234)

> "You want the minimum amount of people working with you that are going to allow you to use the other forms of
> leverage, which I would argue are much more interesting." [RICH-21]

> "Capital means money. To raise money, apply your specific knowledge, with accountability, and show resulting
> good judgment." [TWEET18 #23](https://x.com/naval/status/1002106623639212032)

## Permissioned and permissionless

> "Capital and labor are permissioned leverage. Everyone is chasing capital, but someone has to give it to you.
> Everyone is trying to lead, but someone has to follow you."
> [TWEET18 #25](https://x.com/naval/status/1002106775036874752)

> "Code and media are permissionless leverage. They're the leverage behind the newly rich. You can create
> software and media that works for you while you sleep."
> [TWEET18 #26](https://x.com/naval/status/1002106893265920000)

> "If you can't code, write books and blogs, record videos and podcasts."
> [TWEET18 #28](https://x.com/naval/status/1002107377598873600)

> "Now, you can multiply your efforts without having to involve other humans and without needing money from other
> humans." [RICH-22]

> "This newest form of leverage is where all the new fortunes are made, all the new billionaires." [RICH-22]

**The move:** you can start code and media tonight without asking anyone. Labor and capital come after you've
shown specific knowledge, accountability and judgement (chapter 04).

## Combine them

Tech startups use all of them: the minimum high-output labor (engineers, designers), capital for scaling, and
code and media for distribution. "That is a magic combination, and that's why you see technology startups explode
out of nowhere" [RICH-22].

## Pick a business model with built-in leverage [RICH-24]

| Property | His words |
|---|---|
| Scale economies | "You should try and get into a business where making Widget Number 12 is cheaper than making Widget Number 5" [RICH-24] |
| Zero marginal cost | "Creating another copy of what you just created is free." [RICH-24] |
| Network effects | "A network effect is when each additional user adds value to the existing user base." [RICH-24] |

> "You should always be thinking about how your users, your customers, can add value to each other because that
> is the ultimate form of leverage." [RICH-24]

And on ethics: "If you care about ethics in wealth creation, it is better to create your wealth using code and
media as leverage because then those products are equally available to everybody" [RICH-23].

## The ladder: one industry, rising leverage [RICH-25]

His worked example is real estate. Our summary of the rungs he describes:

| Rung | Specific knowledge | Accountability | Leverage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Day laborer | Little; replaceable | "a faceless cog" [RICH-25] | Tools only |
| General contractor | Some | Takes the job's risk | Labor |
| Property developer | Which lots, which neighbourhoods | More risk | Labor and capital |
| Famous architect or large developer | Deep | A name that adds value | Labor, capital, brand |
| Real estate investment trust | Real estate plus capital markets | High | Capital |
| Real estate tech company | Real estate plus technology | Very high | Code, capital, the best labor |

> "As you layer in more and more kinds of knowledge that can only be gained on the job and aren't common
> knowledge, and you layer in more and more accountability and risk-taking" [RICH-25], the opportunity widens
> from the day laborer to a company that goes public.

## Leverage keeps rising

In 2009: "Rather, I posit that the amount of leverage available to a modern Internet entrepreneur is far, far
greater than was available to entrepreneurs of previous generations." [NAV-the-returns-to-entrepreneurship] And:
"No, people aren't getting any smarter or harder-working. But the amount of leverage is obscene."
[NAV-the-returns-to-entrepreneurship]

In 2026, on AI and programmers: "these are now among the most leveraged people on earth." [NAV-ai] "So now a
programmer with a fleet of AIs is, call it 5-10x more productive than they used to be." [NAV-ai]

> "Intelligence is not normally distributed. Leverage is not normally distributed." [NAV-ai]

> "And remember: there is no demand for average." [NAV-ai]
