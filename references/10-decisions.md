## Why decisions are the job now

> "The quality of our decisions is paramount in the modern age, because we're all leveraged." [NAV-least]

In 2017 he put the advantage in numbers: being right 80% of the time instead of 70% is worth "hundreds of times
more" in the market [TKP-18]. He also says he'd rather set up good systems than rely on individual calls: "I
think our ability to make individual decisions is actually not great." [TKP-18]

## Rule 1 · When in doubt, it's no

This rule is often quoted as *If you can't decide, the answer is no*. That exact wording appears in this corpus
only in the Almanack and in a post by Nivi, so we lead with the versions in Naval's own recorded words:

- **On investing, 2009:** "So, when in doubt, just say no." [VH-angel] His reason, via Buffett: the swings you
  don't make aren't counted against you [VH-angel].
- **On time, 2016,** after citing Derek Sivers's rule of answering only *hell yes* or no: "If I'm not excited
  about something, I'm not going to do it." [TF-QA]
- **On commitments, 2017:** "If I'm not willing to do it right now, then don't do it." [TKP-18] He also says he'd
  like to write a commitment down and check back 48 hours later with a clear mind [TKP-18].
- **On deals, 2026:** "You have to be able to walk away from deals." [NAV-sell] Because deals are long term and
  you're stuck in them [NAV-sell]; he leans toward walking away from suboptimal deals rather than compromising
  [NAV-sell].

**Secondary versions, labelled.** The Almanack's compiled line is "If you cannot decide, the answer is no." [ALM,
compiled by Eric Jorgenson] Its endnote points to a 2018 Periscope we couldn't check. Nivi's 2019 Venture Hacks
post, which he says draws mostly on Naval, makes it lesson 1 for investments [VH-11-lessons, Nivi]. Treat both
as summaries of the rule, not as Naval's verbatim words.

## Rule 2 · Test by doing, when you can

> "The easiest way to figure out if something is viable or not is by doing it. At least do the first step, and the
> second step, and the third, and then decide." [RICH-12]

## Rule 3 · Simplify the model

> "Adding more complexity to your decision-making process gets you a worse answer. You're better off picking the
> single biggest thing or two." [RICH-33]

He warns about false precision from multiplying estimates [RICH-33].

## Rule 4 · Avoid ruin

> "The one thing you have to avoid is the risk of ruin." [RICH-12]

He defines ruin broadly: total loss, prison, or a ruined reputation, all of which wipe you to zero
[NAV-kelly-criterion]. In 2017 he described angel bets as attractive because the downside is capped at 1x while
the upside can be thousands [TKP-18].

## Rule 5 · Slow down when it's big or rushed

> "I employ a 24-hour cooling off period for deals I consider." [ANGEL-2]

He waits again after deciding, before acting [ANGEL-2], and warns that it's easy to overestimate your own
judgement [ANGEL-2].

## Rule 6 · Filter advice before you use it

His questions [RICH-34]: "Is it true? Is it true outside of the context of how that person applied it? Is it true
in my context?" [RICH-34]

## Rule 7 · Get feedback from reality, not people

> "Real feedback comes from free markets and nature." [NAV-fool]

## Rule 8 · Decide from a calm mind

> "In mental terms, stress is an inability to decide what's important." [NAV-peace-motion]

Without peace of mind, he says, you have too many threads running to judge well [NAV-inefficient].

## The tie-breaker, labelled

The Almanack also has a rule for genuine ties: "If you're evenly split on a difficult decision, take the path more
painful in the short term." [ALM, compiled by Eric Jorgenson] We couldn't trace it to a primary source, so it's
here as the compiler's selection, not as a headline rule.

## How to apply it (our reading)

1. **Classify the choice.** Is it testable cheaply (Rule 2) or a long lock-in (Rules 1, 4, 5)?
2. **If testable, test first.** Design the first three steps and decide after them [RICH-12].
3. **If it's a lock-in, write the one or two variables** that matter [RICH-33], and check every outcome for ruin.
4. **Measure excitement now, and again in 48 hours** [TKP-18]. If it's not a clear yes both times, pass.
5. **Log it.** Write what you expect; review in six months (chapter 04).

**Worked scenario (invented).** A founder is offered a $2M distribution deal with a three-year exclusivity
clause. Testable? Partly: she proposes a six-month non-exclusive pilot. Ruin? Exclusivity could lock her out of her
best channel; that's close to ruin for a small company. Excitement at 48 hours: lukewarm. Our reading of his rules:
counter with the pilot, and walk away if exclusivity is non-negotiable [NAV-sell].

**Failure modes (our reading):**
- Using "no when in doubt" to avoid every hard, testable step.
- Building a weighted spreadsheet of twenty factors [RICH-33].
- Deciding on someone else's deadline.
- Taking advice that worked in someone else's context [RICH-34].

**Limits:** he says he doesn't use a checklist system for decisions and doubts they suit entrepreneurs [TKP-18].
Our templates are aids for people who want one; drop them where they slow you down.

**Use it now:** `templates/06-decision-filter.md`, and `templates/09-deal-and-fundraise-check.md` for contracts.

**Checks to run:**
1. Which decision have you been stuck on for weeks? If the answer were no, what would you do instead?
2. Is it a one-way door (years of lock-in) or a step you can test this week?
3. Could any option end in ruin: legal, reputational or total financial loss?
4. Are you deciding calm, or under pressure from someone else's deadline?
