## Why decisions are the job now

> "The quality of our decisions is paramount in the modern age, because we're all leveraged." [NAV-least]

> "So if I can just make one good decision a year, and that makes me all the money that I need for that year,
> then that's perfect." [TF-473]

> "A lot of wisdom involves realizing long-term consequences of your actions." [NAV-ethics]

## Rule 1 · If you can't decide, the answer is no

The famous version is only in the Almanack in this corpus. Eric Jorgenson's endnote points to a 2018 Periscope,
which we don't have:

> "If you cannot decide, the answer is no. And the reason is, modern society is full of options." [ALM, compiled
> by Eric Jorgenson]

> "It's very, very important we only say yes when we are pretty certain." [ALM, compiled by Eric Jorgenson]

His reasoning, as compiled: big choices (a partner, a job, a house, a city, a co-founder) lock you in for years,
and there are far more options than our tribal brains expect [ALM].

The same rule shows up in sources we can check:
- On investing, 2009: "So, when in doubt, just say no." [VH-angel] The reason, from Buffett: "the swings that
  you don't make are not counted against you." [VH-angel]
- On time, 2016, crediting Derek Sivers's rule of answering only hell yes or no: "If I'm not excited about something, I'm not going
  to do it." [TF-QA]

## Rule 2 · When it's a tie, run uphill

> "If you're evenly split on a difficult decision, take the path more painful in the short term." [ALM, compiled
> by Eric Jorgenson]

The compiled reasoning: your brain overweights the option that avoids short-term pain, so the painful path is
probably the one with long-term gain [ALM]. We couldn't trace this passage to a primary source in the corpus.

## Rule 3 · Simplify the model

> "Adding more complexity to your decision-making process gets you a worse answer. You're better off picking the
> single biggest thing or two." [RICH-33]

He warns about false precision from multiplying estimates [RICH-33]. His two variables for a career: "Ask
yourself: What am I really good at, according to observation and people I trust, that the market values?"
[RICH-33]

## Rule 4 · Test by doing

> "The easiest way to figure out if something is viable or not is by doing it. At least do the first step, and the
> second step, and the third, and then decide." [RICH-12]

## Rule 5 · Avoid ruin

> "Don't risk everything. Stay out of jail. Don't bet everything on one big gamble." [NAV-kelly-criterion]

> "The one thing you have to avoid is the risk of ruin." [RICH-12]

## Rule 6 · Slow down when it's big or rushed

> "I employ a 24-hour cooling off period for deals I consider." [ANGEL-2] "Even after I decide to invest, I force
> myself to wait 24 hours before moving forward." [ANGEL-2]

> "It's easy to overestimate your own judgment." [ANGEL-2]

## Rule 7 · Filter advice before you use it

His questions, verbatim [RICH-34]: "Is it true? Is it true outside of the context of how that person applied it? Is
it true in my context?" [RICH-34] Then, do you want to apply it?

## Rule 8 · Get feedback from reality, not people

> "Real feedback comes from free markets and nature." [NAV-fool]

> "Individuals search for truth, groups search for consensus." [NAV-fool]

> "What society wants for you is not always what's good for you." [NAV-consensus]

## Rule 9 · Decide from a calm mind

> "In mental terms, stress is an inability to decide what's important." [NAV-peace-motion]

> "When you don't have peace of mind, it's difficult to make judgments because you have too many threads going
> through your head." [NAV-inefficient]

> "I make decisions much more clearly now, because I can see the long-term outcomes." [NAV-inefficient]

**Our reading:** rules 1, 2 and 6 are filters for one-off, long-lived choices (a co-founder, a job, a big
check). Rules 3 and 4 are for choices you can test cheaply. Use the cheap test first whenever it exists.

**Use it now:** `templates/06-decision-filter.md`.

**Checks to run:**
1. Which decision have you been stuck on for weeks? If the answer were no, what would you do instead?
2. Is it a one-way door (years of lock-in) or a step you can test this week?
3. Could any option end in ruin: legal, reputational or total financial loss?
4. Are you deciding calm, or under pressure from someone else's deadline?
