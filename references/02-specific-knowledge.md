## What it is

> "Specific knowledge is knowledge that you cannot be trained for. If society can train you, it can train
> someone else, and replace you." [TWEET18 #14](https://x.com/naval/status/1002104947624722433)

His 2008 version: "Specific Knowledge = Knowing how to do something that society cannot yet easily train other
people to do" [NAV-life-formulas-i]

> "And it's not true that everything can be taught. In fact, the most interesting things cannot be taught."
> [RICH-13] "But everything can be learned." [RICH-13]

Why "specific" and not "unique": "it can only be built as part of a larger obsession, interest, and time spent
in that domain." [RICH-14]

## You mostly find it, you don't choose it

> "Especially if you're later in life, let's say your post 20, 21, 22, you almost don't get to choose which
> specific knowledge you have." [RICH-13]

> "You almost have to look back on your own life and see what you're actually good at." [RICH-13]

> "So, very often, your specific knowledge is observed and often observed by other people who know you well and
> revealed in situations rather than something that you come up with." [RICH-13]

His own case: he wanted to be a scientist, but his mother, watching him critique how the local pizza parlour
sold its slices, told him at 15 or 16 he'd go into business [RICH-13]. Looking back, "all of this stuff feels
like play to me, but it looks like work to others." [RICH-13]

In 2025 he adds that you find it by doing. A friend who will call 400 people to get one yes: "So that's his
specific knowledge. It is knowledge." [NAV-find] And "when you are working for yourself, you'll also naturally
tend to pick things and do things in a way that aligns with who you are and what your specific knowledge is."
[NAV-find]

## Go all in, or lose to someone who does

> "So, if you're not 100% into it somebody else who is 100% into it will outperform you." [RICH-13]

> "If you're going to be the top at it, you have to be almost psychopathic level at which you enjoy the thing."
> [NAV-enjoy]

## Don't assemble it too deliberately

> "I actually think the best way is just to follow your own obsession." [RICH-14]

> "But I think if you go around trying to build it a little too deliberately, if you become too goal-oriented
> on the money, then you won't pick the right thing." [RICH-14]

On combining skills (the skill-stack idea, which Nivi credits to Scott Adams [RICH-14]): "So, it's much
easier to be top 5 percentile at three or four things than it is to be literally the number one at something."
[RICH-14] And: "So, whatever you are a natural at, you want to double down on that." [RICH-14]

## Apprenticeship, and the steepest learning curve

> "To the extent that specific knowledge is taught, it's on the job. It's through apprenticeships." [RICH-14]

His example is Warren Buffett offering to work for Benjamin Graham for free, and Graham's reply as he tells it:
"Actually, you're overpriced, free is overpriced." [RICH-14]

If you can't afford an apprenticeship: "Find the part of the job with the steepest learning curve."
[NAV-finding-time]

> "You build specific knowledge by taking accountability for things that other people don't know how to do."
> [NAV-finding-time]

## Timely and timeless

> "There are two forms of specific knowledge: timely and timeless." [NAV-finding-time]

> "Timeless specific knowledge usually can't be taught, and it sticks with you forever. Timely specific
> knowledge comes and goes; but it tends to have a fairly long shelf life." [NAV-finding-time]

His examples: machine learning, learned out of real interest just as it takes off, is timely; persuasion is
timeless [NAV-finding-time]. On where timely knowledge lives: "Technology will always be a great field where you
can pick up specific knowledge that is valuable to society." [NAV-finding-time]

**A tell:** "Another good indicator of specific knowledge is when someone can't give a straight answer to the
question" of what they do every day [NAV-finding-time].

## The Internet makes a niche big enough

> "The Internet has massively broadened the possible space of careers. Most people haven't figured this out
> yet." [TWEET18 #8](https://x.com/naval/status/1002103832879419392)

> "So, whatever niche obsession you have, the internet allows you to scale." [RICH-10] "but if you just want to
> reach 50,000 passionate people like you, there's an audience out there for you." [RICH-10]

**Checks to run:**
1. What do you do that feels like play to you and looks like work to others? Ask three people who know you well.
2. Could a course teach someone your job in six months? If yes, what part of it couldn't they learn that way?
3. Which of your skills are timely, which are timeless, and which pair could you combine?
4. Is there an audience of 50,000 people who care about your niche? Where do they gather?
