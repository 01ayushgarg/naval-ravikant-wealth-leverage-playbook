## The most important tweet, in his view

> "You're not going to get rich renting out your time. You must own equity - a piece of a business - to gain your
> financial freedom." [TWEET18 #5](https://x.com/naval/status/1002103670400417792)

In 2020, asked which tweet matters most: "So I think that is the most important foundational tweet." [TF-473]

## Why renting out your time doesn't get you there

> "But the most basic is just that your inputs are very closely tied to your outputs." [RICH-06]

> "So, what that means is when you're sleeping, you're not earning. When you're retired, you're not earning."
> [RICH-06]

> "And you're essentially replaceable because you're now doing a set role." [RICH-06]

The contrast he draws: one great engineer's hour can be worth billions, while "the best lumberjack in the world
may be like 3x better than one of the worst lumberjacks" [RICH-06].

> "You really just want a job, or a career, or a profession where your inputs don't match your outputs."
> [RICH-06]

## Equity is the risk position, so it gets the upside

He walks down the capital stack: employees are paid first, then lenders, then owners [RICH-20].

> "The equity holders take on greater risk, but in exchange, they get nearly unlimited upside." [RICH-20]

How you get offered it: whoever decides your pay is asking how replaceable you are, and with high
accountability "they have to give you equity, which is a piece of the upside." [RICH-20]

## Many ways to own a piece

> "So everybody who really makes money at some point owns a piece of a product, or a business, or some kind of
> IP." [RICH-06]

> "That can be through stock options, so you can be working at a tech company. That's a fine way to start."
> [RICH-06]

From 2020: "You could be an employee of a startup where you have stock options, but if you don't own a piece of
a business, it's going to be extremely hard to get wealthy." [TF-473]

| Route [ANGEL-1] | What it takes | His caution |
|---|---|---|
| Start a company | Most equity | "Being a founder your entire life is a very tough road. Most people do not have the constitution for it." [ANGEL-1] |
| Join as a proven operator | A reputation for execution | "Another classic route is to get recognized as an extremely competent execution person, so you get the call when the next successful company's scaling." [ANGEL-1] |
| Invest | Capital, judgement, dealflow (chapter 12) | "As the hits become bigger and bigger and the returns become more nonlinear, it makes more sense to play as an investor and a little less sense to play as a founder." [ANGEL-1] |

## Think like an owner before you are one

> "The difference between a founder and an employee is the difference between a principal and an agent."
> [NAV-principal-agent]

> "Train yourself how to think like a principal, and eventually you will become a principal."
> [NAV-principal-agent]

> "If you think like the owner and you act like the owner, it's only a matter of time until you become the
> owner." [NAV-principal-agent]

On what founders want from early staff: "The hardest thing for any founder is finding employees with a founder
mentality. This is a fancy way of saying they care enough." [NAV-finding-time] And if you're the employee: "The
knowledge and skills you gain by developing a founder mentality set you up to be a founder down the line;
that's your compensation." [NAV-finding-time]

## If you're the owner: share it

> "You want to be generous with your top lieutenants" [NAV-principal-agent]

From 2011, on pre-traction startups: "Your first two engineers? They're just late founders. Treat them as
such." [NAV-why-you-cant-hire] "Close the equity gap, and hiring will get a lot easier."
[NAV-why-you-cant-hire] (His percentages in that post are 2011 Silicon Valley numbers. Treat them as dated.)

**Use it now:** the "inputs vs outputs" and "equity" rows of `templates/03-hourly-rate-and-time-audit.md`.

**Checks to run:**
1. If you stopped working for a year, what would still pay you? That's your equity.
2. Do your outputs scale with your hours, or can one good hour be worth a thousand?
3. Which route to ownership fits you: founder, early operator, or investor?
4. If you employ people, who acts like a principal? Do they own like one?
