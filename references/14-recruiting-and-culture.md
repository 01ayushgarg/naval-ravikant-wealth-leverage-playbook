# 14 · Recruiting and culture

Sources in this chapter: NAV-the-80-hour-myth (29 Nov 2005) · NAV-why-you-cant-hire (13 Dec 2011) ·
NAV-build-a-team-that-ships (27 Apr 2012) · RICH-11 (22 Mar 2019) · NAV-curate-people (7 Nov 2025, a podcast
with Nivi; only Naval's lines are quoted) · NAV-sell (11 May 2026) · NAV-industrial (1 Jun 2026).

His 2025 episode on recruiting opens with a line Nivi attributes to another investor, that the team you build is
the company you build [NAV-curate-people, Nivi]. Naval agrees, and says it's truest for the first people a
founder manages directly: "they're the DNA of the company." [NAV-curate-people]

## Why recruiting comes first

> "Recruiting is the most important thing because you need creativity; you need motivated people."
> [NAV-curate-people]

His non-obvious reason: "the best people truly only want to work with the best people." [NAV-curate-people] Weaker
colleagues are a cognitive load on them, and remind them they belong somewhere else [NAV-curate-people].

## The founder can't outsource it

> "So we really cannot outsource recruiting." [NAV-curate-people]

Nobody else will be as selective as a founder, he says, and recruiters and HR can't bring you unusual people
[NAV-curate-people]. He also argues against hiring "people who are better than you" early on: they won't stay
long, because early on all you bring is yourself [NAV-curate-people]. His conclusion: "And the clearest way you
can show how good you are is by recruiting great people." [NAV-curate-people]

## What to look for

- **Intelligence, energy, integrity**, Buffett's test, plus one he adds, low ego: "Low-ego people are just much
  easier to manage." [NAV-curate-people] He says you can manage 30 or 40 of them where you might manage five
  high-ego people [NAV-curate-people]. The three-part test is the same one he gives for partners [RICH-11].
- **Self-motivated.** People who have to be pushed and flogged "don't belong in an early-stage startup."
  [NAV-curate-people]
- **Taste, matched to the domain.** "Makers have taste in other makers." [NAV-curate-people] Someone hiring for
  social media should have a great account themselves [NAV-curate-people].
- **Undiscovered.** By the time someone is famous online or fully pedigreed, he says, they're very hard to
  recruit; a good sourcer hunts talent before everyone else sees it [NAV-curate-people].

## The bar: a test you can run today

His test: tell a candidate to walk into the team room, pick anyone at random, interview them for 30 minutes, and
not join if they aren't impressed. If you flinch at the idea of one particular person being picked, "That's the
person you need to let go." [NAV-curate-people] His company motto, which he calls aspirational, is "Geniuses only"
[NAV-curate-people], and he says not to hire to fill a slot: if they're not a genius, don't hire them
[NAV-curate-people].

## Break rules to get them

> "Every single hire, we had to break some core rule of recruiting." [NAV-curate-people]

He lists objections he'll work around, such as a new baby, a university post or not being able to afford to
exercise options, and says founders can break rules on the cap table, roles and title [NAV-curate-people]. His
2011 version for pre-traction startups was to treat the first engineers as late founders
[NAV-why-you-cant-hire].

## Culture: small, odd, opinionated

> "Early teams do look like cults." [NAV-curate-people]

He says mixing too many kinds of people regresses the culture to a bland average [NAV-curate-people], and that
great founders are highly opinionated. "It just takes a small group of people to create something great."
[NAV-curate-people] His 2012 rules point the same way: small team, all doers, one person accountable per project
[NAV-build-a-team-that-ships].

**Lead, don't manage.** "Management is telling people what to do, and leadership is making them want to do it."
[NAV-sell] He says that has to be real: find where what they want overlaps with what you need [NAV-sell].

## Letting people go

> "If you're not firing, it means that you're deluding yourself." [NAV-curate-people]

He admits he's historically been bad at firing, and that what works for him is being convinced the person will
be more effective elsewhere, then helping them get there [NAV-sell]. From 2005: a mediocre, reliable performer
damages a startup more than anything [NAV-the-80-hour-myth].

## In the AI era

In 2026 he says that someone who is very good with AI, smart and creative is more hireable than ever, "for the
leverage." [NAV-industrial] See chapter 17.

## How to apply it (our reading)

1. **Write the capabilities** you need, not job titles [NAV-curate-people].
2. **Source before you need to.** Keep a list of unusually good people you meet, in any role.
3. **Score every candidate** on intelligence, energy, integrity, low ego, self-motivation and domain taste, with
   evidence you've seen (`templates/08-hiring-scorecard.md`).
4. **Run the random-interview test** on your current team once a quarter.
5. **Make the offer fit the person:** remove their specific objection rather than offering the standard package.

**Worked scenario (invented).** A six-person startup needs "a marketer". Instead of interviewing ten marketers,
the founder lists capabilities: writes clearly about developer tools, can run experiments, understands pricing. A
customer who writes a sharp weekly blog about the product scores high on taste and self-motivation, low on
title. The objection: she can't take a pay cut while her partner finishes school. The founder offers more cash,
less equity, and remote work for a year. Six months later she runs growth.

**Failure modes (our reading):**
- Delegating early hiring to a recruiter.
- Hiring the best of a weak slate to fill a slot.
- Keeping a pleasant, mediocre performer to avoid a hard talk.
- Copying a big company's culture handbook at ten people.

**Limits:** "geniuses only" is, in his word, aspirational, and his rule-breaking assumes a founder with room to
negotiate. Employment law varies; check before you change terms or let anyone go.

**Use it now:** `templates/08-hiring-scorecard.md`.

**Checks to run:**
1. Who in your team would you flinch at a candidate interviewing at random?
2. Who did the last hire, you or someone else? Would you have hired the same person?
3. What objection stopped your best candidate this year, and could you have removed it?
4. Is your team getting more uniform in purpose, or more average?
