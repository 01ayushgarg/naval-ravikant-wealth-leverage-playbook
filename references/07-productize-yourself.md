## Two words for the whole thread

On the podcast, Nivi says to Naval: "You summarized this entire tweetstorm with two words" [RICH-37, Nivi].
The two words are *Productize yourself*. They aren't one of the 40 tweets, but he had posted them eight years
earlier: "For success, do what you love. For money, use leverage. People love themselves. Products create leverage.
Productize yourself." [X 2010-09-29](https://x.com/naval/status/25854742061) Three days after the thread he quoted
it with "Summary: Productize Yourself." [X 2018-06-03](https://x.com/naval/status/1003356436091400192) Naval's
unpacking:

> "Productize has specific knowledge and leverage. Yourself has uniqueness and accountability." [RICH-37]

His two questions, paraphrased [RICH-37]: is this authentic to me, and am I scaling it, with labor, capital, code
or media? In 2020: "you want to productize yourself into a business, and then you want to own that business."

## Learn to sell, learn to build

> "Learn to sell. Learn to build. If you can do both, you will be unstoppable."
> [TWEET18 #12](https://x.com/naval/status/1002104154737684480)

Both words are broad [RICH-15]. Building can be design, development, manufacturing, logistics or running a
service; selling "can mean marketing, it can mean communicating, it can mean recruiting, it can mean raising
money" [RICH-15].

**Which first?** "When you're starting out, when you're trying to be recognized, building is better." [RICH-15]
But "sales skills actually scale better over time." [RICH-15] If you build but dislike selling in person, he
points to writing, which is easier to learn [RICH-15]. Chapter 15 covers how he sells.

## Be the best in the world at something you define

The thread says to become the best in the world at what you do, and keep redefining what you do until that's true
([#37](https://x.com/naval/status/1002108897551773697)). Why it pays to be the best: "The best in the world at
anything gets to do it for everyone." [X 2019-10-10](https://x.com/naval/status/1182090706954981377)

> "Keep changing your objective until it arrives at your specific knowledge, skill sets, position, capabilities,
> location and interests." [RICH-30]

In 2026, with AI raising the bar: "However, the set of things you can be best at is infinite." [NAV-ai]

## Escape competition through authenticity

The heading is his own post from two months before the thread: "Escape competition through authenticity."
[X 2018-03-20](https://x.com/naval/status/975975798204112896)

> "If you are building and marketing something that's an extension of who you are, no one can compete with you."
> [RICH-31]

But authenticity has to meet a market: an empty market can be a warning sign [RICH-31]. From 2011, he observed
that the best companies are often an extension of the founder's personality [VH-passion-market].

**A cautionary tale: Epinions [RICH-32].** His first startup, a product-review site, got pulled into a fight with
its peers over comparison shopping while a big retailer won. "We got the booby prize because we were caught up in
competition with a bunch of our peers." [RICH-32]

## Pick the channel that's natural to you

He says marketing yourself is a matching problem: do what's natural to you [NAV-enjoy]. His menu: talking suits
a podcast, long-form writing a newsletter or book, short-form writing X, and video makers video [NAV-enjoy]. And
you find it by iterating, then pausing to reflect on how well it did [NAV-reflect].

## How to apply it (our reading)

1. **Answer his two questions** for your main project [RICH-37]. If either answer is weak, fix that first.
2. **Define your niche narrowly** enough that you're close to best in the world at it.
3. **Score yourself as builder and seller**, 1 to 5. Raise the lower one by one point this quarter.
4. **Pick one channel** that you'd enjoy with no audience, and ship weekly for 12 weeks.
5. **Reflect every four weeks** [NAV-reflect]: what got pull, what didn't, redefine the niche.

**Worked scenario (invented).** Ana is a freelance copywriter competing on price on gig sites. Redefined niche:
*onboarding emails for developer tools*, where she has three years of results. Builder skill: 4 (she writes
well). Seller skill: 2. Channel: a weekly teardown newsletter, since she likes long-form writing. After 12
weeks: 700 subscribers, four inbound clients at twice her old rate, and a $79 template pack that sells while she
sleeps. The newsletter is her selling.

**Failure modes (our reading):**
- Copying a successful creator's format. That's competing, not being yourself [RICH-31].
- A niche so narrow there's no market [RICH-31].
- Building and never selling, or the reverse.
- Judging a channel after three weeks.
