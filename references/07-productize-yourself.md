## Learn to sell, learn to build

> "Learn to sell. Learn to build. If you can do both, you will be unstoppable."
> [TWEET18 #12](https://x.com/naval/status/1002104154737684480)

Both words are broad [RICH-15]. Building can be design, development, manufacturing, logistics or running a
service. Selling "can mean marketing, it can mean communicating, it can mean recruiting, it can mean raising
money" [RICH-15].

> "There's a builder and there's a seller." [RICH-15] "The ultimate is when one individual can do both."
> [RICH-15]

### Which first?

> "When you're starting out, when you're trying to be recognized, building is better." [RICH-15]

> "So, sales skills actually scale better over time." [RICH-15]

> "So, I think if you only had to pick up one, you can start with building and then transition to selling."
> [RICH-15]

If you build but hate selling in person: "And writing is a skill that can be learned much more easily than,
say, in-person selling" [RICH-15].

## Be the best in the world at something you define

> "Become the best in the world at what you do. Keep redefining what you do until this is true."
> [TWEET18 #37](https://x.com/naval/status/1002108897551773697)

> "You can literally get paid for just being you." [RICH-30]

> "Keep changing your objective until it arrives at your specific knowledge, skill sets, position, capabilities,
> location and interests." [RICH-30]

> "But the most important thing for any entrepreneur is to find founder-product-market fit" [RICH-30]

In 2026, with AI raising the bar: "However, the set of things you can be best at is infinite." [NAV-ai]

## Escape competition through authenticity

> "No one can compete with you on being you." [RICH-10]

> "If you are building and marketing something that's an extension of who you are, no one can compete with you."
> [RICH-31]

> "In entrepreneurship, the masses are never right." [RICH-31]

But authenticity has to meet a market: "if the whole market is empty, that can be a warning indicator." [RICH-31]

> "Ideally, you want to end up specializing in being you." [RICH-31]

From 2011: "Often, the best companies are ones where the product is an extension of the founder's personality,
which shouldn't be a big surprise, since everyone is passionate about themselves." [VH-passion-market]

## A cautionary tale: Epinions [RICH-32]

His first startup was a product-review site. It got pulled into the comparison-shopping fight with its peers,
and Amazon won retail. "We got the booby prize because we were caught up in competition with a bunch of our
peers." [RICH-32] What they should have done, in his words: follow what the consumer wanted, which was reviews of
scarce, local things. "You're playing a stupid game. You're going to win a stupid prize." [RICH-32]

## Pick the channel that's natural to you

On marketing yourself in 2025: "But you have to do what is very natural to you." [NAV-enjoy] "It is a fit
problem. It is a matching problem." [NAV-enjoy] His menu: if you like talking, a podcast; long-form
writing, a newsletter or a book; short-form writing, X; making video, video [NAV-enjoy].

And how you find it: iterate, reflect, "process of iteration until you figure it out" [NAV-reflect].
