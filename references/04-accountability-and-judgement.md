# 04 · Accountability and judgement

He spells it *judgement* in the 2018 tweets and *judgment* on nav.al. Quotes keep his spelling.

## Part 1 · Accountability

The thread's version: embrace accountability and take business risks under your own name, and society rewards
you with responsibility, equity and leverage [TWEET18 #19](https://x.com/naval/status/1002105652792066048).

> "So accountability is a double-edged thing. It allows you to take credit when things go well and to bear the
> brunt of the failure when things go badly." [RICH-19]

### Why it pays

> "Essentially, taking accountability for your actions is the same as taking an equity position in all of your
> work." [RICH-20]

He calls it "reputational skin in the game" [RICH-20], and says high accountability makes you less replaceable
[RICH-20].

### What it costs

> "You have to be willing to let people criticize you." [NAV-accountability]

He says people who can fail in public under their own names gain a lot of power [RICH-19], and that he was
nervous when he started talking about philosophy under his own name around 2014 [RICH-19]. Today, in his view,
the risk to a name is more about integrity than about economic failure [RICH-20].

### You can start today, inside a job

> "Accountability is something you can take on immediately." [NAV-finding-time]

His example: offer to own the problem nobody wants, such as a factory owner's fundraising, and you can become the
heir apparent [NAV-finding-time]. You don't have to quit; he says you can apply accountability, leverage and
specific knowledge within your existing career [NAV-accountability]. Make your role visible, and keep giving out
credit [NAV-finding-time].

Teams need it too: "a really, really well-functioning team is small and has clear accountability for each of the
different portions." [RICH-19] His 2005 warning: "Nothing damages a startup like a mediocre and reliable
performer." [NAV-the-80-hour-myth]

## Part 2 · Judgement

The thread says leverage is a force multiplier for your judgement, and that judgement needs experience but can be
built faster through foundational skills ([#29](https://x.com/naval/status/1002107570692112384),
[#30](https://x.com/naval/status/1002107679353942016)).

> "In an age of infinite leverage, judgment becomes the most important skill." [RICH-26]

### What it is, and why small edges matter

He defines wisdom as knowing the long-term consequences of your actions, and judgment as wisdom applied to
external problems [RICH-26]. On the payoff, in 2017:

> "In fact, someone who makes decisions right 80% of the time instead of 70% of the time will be valued and
> compensated in the market hundreds of times more." [TKP-18]

In 2020 he made the same point with Warren Buffett at 85% against competitors at 70% [NAV-least].

### The sequence: hustle for leverage, then slow down

> "Once you have the leverage, then you wanna slow down a bit, because your judgment really matters." [RICH-26]

### How it's built, and what ruins it

He says intellect without experience is often worse than useless, and that the reps are tries, not hours:
"you take 10,000 tries at something." [RICH-26] In 2025: "You start with reasoning and then you build up your
judgment." [NAV-arena] He frames good judgement partly as avoiding bad calls: "It's about avoiding the
incorrect judgments." [TKP-18] What ruins it is emotion: "The more outraged somebody is, I guarantee you, the
worse their judgment is." [RICH-26]

### You can't grade your own

> "your ability to assess your own judgment is subject to your level of judgment." [ANGEL-2]

His fix: don't ask people whether your judgement is good, since polite people say yes; ask about your
weaknesses [ANGEL-2].

## How to apply it (our reading)

1. **Pick one thing to own this month** with your name on it, ideally the problem nobody wants
   [NAV-finding-time].
2. **Write the call before the outcome.** For each decision that matters, log what you expect and why.
3. **Score the log every quarter.** Your hit rate is your judgement, not your confidence.
4. **Slow down in proportion to leverage.** The more people, capital or code a decision moves, the longer the
   cooling-off [RICH-26].
5. **Ask for weaknesses**, not ratings, from two people who'll be honest [ANGEL-2].

**Worked number (invented).** A product lead logs 20 calls in a quarter and gets 13 right: 65%. Each call
steers about $200,000 of engineering time. Moving to 75% means two more good calls a quarter, about $400,000 of
work not wasted. This is our arithmetic on his 70% against 80% point [TKP-18].

**Failure modes (our reading):**
- Taking credit without taking risk. That's status, not accountability.
- Owning so much that nothing ships. Accountability is for outcomes, not for every task.
- Grading judgement by results in a lucky year. Use the log, not the bank balance.
- Deciding big things while angry [RICH-26].

**Limits:** putting your name on work is safer in some jobs and countries than others. Weigh the downside before
going public (chapter 18).

**Checks to run:**

3. Which decision of yours was most leveraged this year? Did you slow down for it?
4. Who will tell you your weaknesses? Have you asked them?
