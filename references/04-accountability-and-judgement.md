He spells it *judgement* in the 2018 tweets and *judgment* on nav.al. Quotes keep his spelling.

## Part 1 · Accountability

> "Embrace accountability, and take business risks under your own name. Society will reward you with
> responsibility, equity, and leverage." [TWEET18 #19](https://x.com/naval/status/1002105652792066048)

> "So accountability is a double-edged thing. It allows you to take credit when things go well and to bear the
> brunt of the failure when things go badly." [RICH-19]

> "Without accountability, you don't have incentives. Without accountability, you can't build credibility."
> [RICH-19]

### Why it pays

> "If you have high accountability, that makes you less replaceable." [RICH-20]

> "Essentially, taking accountability for your actions is the same as taking an equity position in all of your
> work." [RICH-20]

> "I think of accountability as reputational skin in the game." [RICH-20]

### What it costs

> "You have to be willing to let people criticize you." [NAV-accountability]

> "The people who have the ability to fail in public under their own names actually gain a lot of power"
> [RICH-19]

His own example: "Only around 2014, 2015 did I start talking about philosophy and psychological things and
broader things." [RICH-19] "It made me a little nervous because I was doing it under my own name." [RICH-19]

The real risk today, in his view: "I think these days the accountability risk with a name happens more around
integrity, rather than it does around purely economic failure." [RICH-20] And a limit on fame: "It's better to
be anonymous and rich than to be poor and famous" [RICH-19].

### Teams need it too

> "a really, really well-functioning team is small and has clear accountability for each of the different
> portions." [RICH-19]

From 2005: "Lastly, be ruthless on accountability and output over the long term. Nothing damages a startup like
a mediocre and reliable performer." [NAV-the-80-hour-myth]

### You can start today, inside a job

> "Accountability is something you can take on immediately." [NAV-finding-time]

His example: offer to own the thing nobody wants, such as the owner's fundraising problem at a factory, and you
can become the heir apparent [NAV-finding-time]. He also says you don't have to quit: "You can start applying
accountability, leverage and specific knowledge within your existing career." [NAV-accountability]

Make the output visible. "People need to be able to tell what role you had in the company's success."
[NAV-finding-time] But: "You always want to be giving out credit." [NAV-finding-time]

## Part 2 · Judgement

> "Leverage is a force multiplier for your judgement."
> [TWEET18 #29](https://x.com/naval/status/1002107570692112384)

> "Judgement requires experience, but can be built faster by learning foundational skills."
> [TWEET18 #30](https://x.com/naval/status/1002107679353942016)

> "In an age of infinite leverage, judgment becomes the most important skill." [RICH-26]

### What it is

> "My definition of wisdom is knowing the long term consequences of your actions" [RICH-26] "Wisdom applied to
> external problems I think is judgment." [RICH-26]

### Why small edges are worth a lot

> "If Warren Buffett makes the right decision 85% of the time and his competitors get it right 70% of the time,
> Buffett will win everything." [NAV-least]

> "Obviously, hard work is not the solution. Good decision making and high leverage is the solution."
> [NAV-least]

### The sequence: hustle for leverage, then slow down

> "Once you have the leverage, then you wanna slow down a bit, because your judgment really matters." [RICH-26]

> "Demonstrated judgment, credibility around the judgment, is so critical." [RICH-26]

### How it's built

> "Intellect without any experience is often worse than useless" [RICH-26]

> "If you are smart and you iterate fast, it's not even you put 10,000 hours into something, but you take 10,000
> tries at something." [RICH-26]

> "You start with reasoning and then you build up your judgment." [NAV-arena]

### What ruins it

> "the people with the best judgment are actually among the least emotional." [RICH-26]

> "The more outraged somebody is, I guarantee you, the worse their judgment is." [RICH-26]

### You can't grade your own

> "your ability to assess your own judgment is subject to your level of judgment." [ANGEL-2]

> "Don't ask them if you have good judgment or not, because polite people will always tell you yes. Rather, ask
> them about your weaknesses." [ANGEL-2]

**The move** (our reading): accountability is the one input you can raise this week. It earns the credibility
that brings capital and people, and the reps that build judgement.

**Use it now:** rows 3 and 4 of `templates/02-leverage-audit.md`, and the decision log in
`templates/06-decision-filter.md`.

**Checks to run:**
1. What at work has your name on it, alone? What could you volunteer to own this month?
2. Could anyone outside your team tell what you contributed last quarter?
3. Which decision of yours was most leveraged this year? Did you slow down for it?
4. Who will tell you your weaknesses? Have you asked them?
