This chapter covers happiness only as it bears on building: decisions, drive, agency. His wider philosophy is out
of scope.

## The thread ends here

> "When you're finally wealthy, you'll realize that it wasn't what you were seeking in the first place. But that's
> for another day." [TWEET18 #40](https://x.com/naval/status/1002109558058237953)

> "The first thing you realize when you've made a bunch of money is that you're still the same person." [RICH-35]

His favourite tweet, he says: "A fit body, a calm mind, a house full of love." [RICH-35] Those, he argues, can't
be bought; they have to be earned [RICH-35].

From 2020: "And one of the things you learn after you make money is that money doesn't make you happier."
[TF-473] But he won't pretend money doesn't help: people "know money can remove a lot of sources of unhappiness
and get them to a point where happiness is under their control." [NAV-consensus]

His 2008 formula: "Happiness = Health + Wealth + Good Relationships" [NAV-life-formulas-i]

## Desire is a contract

> "Desire's a contract you make to be unhappy until you get what you want." [NAV-desire]

> "So happiness is returning to the state where nothing is missing in this moment." [NAV-desire]

A practical test from 2016: ask, "Is it so important to me that I be unhappy unless this goes my way?" [TF-QA]

## Happiness is a skill

> "The first step to increasing your level of happiness is realizing you can." [NAV-skill]

> "So, happiness is a skill like nutrition and fitness." [NAV-skill]

From 2016: "the most important trick to be happy is to realize that happiness is a skill that you develop and a
choice that you make." [TF-QA] One method he names: "I try to break a bad habit every six months" [TF-QA].

## The objection: won't it cost me my drive?

The objection he hears from smart overachievers and entrepreneurs: that being happier would cost them their
drive [NAV-smart].

> "You're not smart because you're unhappy; you're unhappy because you're smart." [NAV-smart]

> "At the same time, being unhappy is very inefficient." [NAV-inefficient]

> "If you're a driven, unhappy person, your mind will be on 24/7." [NAV-inefficient]

> "I've become more productive even though I don't work as hard, because I make better decisions."
> [NAV-inefficient]

> "Being happy will actually increase your effectiveness. As long as the increase in effectiveness is higher than
> the reduction in drive, you're well off." [NAV-least]

> "Would you rather be the best in the world by working the hardest for it or by working the least for it,
> because you worked the most intelligently?" [NAV-least]

Tim Ferriss read him a tweet in 2020 that says the same: "Imagine how effective you would be if you weren't
anxious all the time." [TF-473, Tim Ferriss reading his tweet]

## Peace is the goal

> "We say peace of mind, but what we really want is peace from mind." [NAV-peace]

> "Peace is happiness at rest; happiness is peace in motion." [NAV-peace-motion]

> "In mental terms, stress is an inability to decide what's important." [NAV-peace-motion]

## Health first, and his own limit

> "Physical health is the foundation of everything." [NAV-material]

His caveat: "Although physical health is the most important, I speak the least about it because it's the area
where I have the least specific knowledge." [NAV-material]

And on renouncing money instead: "You can achieve happiness without material comfort, and it probably would be a
more lasting form of happiness. But that's playing on hard mode." [NAV-material]

## Indirect, like wealth

> "if you want to be happy, you minimize yourself and you engage in high flow activities" [NAV-indirect]

**The move** (our reading): treat calm as an input to judgement, not a reward for later. The chapters on
leverage and decisions only work if the person deciding can think clearly.

**Use it now:** the "peace check" at the end of `templates/03-hourly-rate-and-time-audit.md`.

**Checks to run:**
1. Which desire is costing you the most peace right now? Is it worth being unhappy until you get it?
2. Has anxiety made a recent decision worse? What would you have decided calm?
3. Where are you blaming the market, a partner or luck for something you could act on?
4. When did you last protect health over work, and what did it cost the work?
