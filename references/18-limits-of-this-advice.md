# 18 · The limits of this advice

Sources in this chapter: NAV-unquantifiable-risk (29 Nov 2005) · TF-097 (18 Aug 2015) · TKP-18 (27 Feb 2017) ·
RICH-04 (6 Mar 2019) · RICH-13 (25 Mar 2019) · RICH-33 (19 May 2019) · NAV-accountability (21 Jun 2019) ·
NAV-material (29 Jan 2020) · ANGEL-1 (17 Nov 2019) · NAV-agency (25 Aug 2025).

Most of this chapter is our reading. Where he names a limit himself, we cite him; where the limit is ours, we
say so.

## 1 · Survivorship: we hear from the winners

He named the bias himself in 2005, about social startups that looked easy from the outside: "However, this is just
survivor bias at work." [NAV-unquantifiable-risk] The failures, he wrote, are far more common and not obvious from
looking at the winners [NAV-unquantifiable-risk]. In 2017 he was sceptical of success stories in general: "I think
there's ex-post facto reasoning and origin stories that people make up about why they were successful." [TKP-18]
He said he'd rather look at who is consistently successful, since Silicon Valley has lots of one-hit wonders
[TKP-18].

**Our reading:** the same applies to him. A playbook built from one successful person's words can't show how many
people followed the same rules and didn't get rich. His own answer is to aim for the outcome that holds in most
worlds, not the lucky one [RICH-04], and his claim that luck matters less over longer timeframes and with more
iterations [NAV-agency]. That is a claim, not data.

## 2 · Place and industry

- **Tech hubs.** On angel investing: "A lot of this will not translate to other locations." [ANGEL-1] In 2015
  he advised anyone in a tech business to move to Silicon Valley [TF-097].
- **The Internet.** He says the message has a pro-Internet bias because of how it's delivered
  [NAV-accountability].
- **Our reading for other fields:** permissionless leverage (code and media) is weakest where the product is
  physical, licensed or in-person: trades, care, clinical work, hospitality. There, his real-estate ladder
  (chapter 03) is the better map: specific knowledge, then accountability under your name, then labor and capital,
  then brand.

## 3 · Starting points differ

He describes himself as starting as a poor kid in India and concludes that anybody can make it, "in that sense"
[RICH-04]. **Our reading:** his path ran through Silicon Valley during a long technology boom, which he
advises others to move to [TF-097]. Savings, visas, caring duties, health and local law all change which moves are
safe. Never take a risk that could be ruin for you, even if it wasn't for him (chapter 10).

## 4 · It isn't a formula

He warns that these ideas aren't math, and that you can't carry variables around and get absolute outputs
[RICH-33]. He says most people over about 20 don't get to choose their specific knowledge [RICH-13]. And he says he
doesn't use a checklist system for decisions, and doubts it's great for investors or entrepreneurs [TKP-18].
**Our reading:** our templates are aids, not the method. If a template slows you down, drop it.

## 5 · Where he says his knowledge is thin

He calls health the area where he has the least specific knowledge [NAV-material]. **Our reading:** treat his
health and happiness lines as one person's practice, not medical advice.

## How to use these limits (our suggestion)

1. **Before acting on any chapter, name your context:** country, industry, savings runway, dependants.
2. **Ask his own advice filter** of every idea: is it true, is it true outside his context, is it true in mine
   (chapter 10, Rule 6)?
3. **Size the bet to survive being wrong.** If the advice fails for you, can you recover in a year?
4. **Look for base rates,** not stories: how many people in your field tried this, and how many succeeded?

**Checks to run:**
1. Which chapter's advice depends most on being in tech or a tech hub? Does that apply to you?
2. What's your runway if a move from this playbook fails?
3. Whose failure stories have you heard, not just success stories, in the path you're considering?
4. Which template would you drop because it doesn't fit how you decide?
