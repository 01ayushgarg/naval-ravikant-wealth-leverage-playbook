## Selling is credibility

> "Credibility is way more important than sales." [NAV-sell]

He opens the 2026 episode by saying he doesn't really sell; he works out what he believes and conveys it as
accurately and honestly as he can [NAV-sell]. His reason: "the people you most want to impress in life are the ones
who can see right through you." [NAV-sell] His picture of credibility is the real estate agent who steers you away
from bad houses, so you trust them when the right one appears [NAV-sell].

## Honesty first, then kindness

> "Charisma is the ability to project confidence and love simultaneously—or just power and good intentions."
> [NAV-sell]

If he has to pick, honesty wins over kindness, but he says you can usually do both, and that being honest without
kindness means people won't listen [NAV-sell]. He calls his version "selfish honesty": being objective so he makes
the right decisions for himself [NAV-sell].

## Sell what you believe

> "If it feels to you like you're selling, then you're probably selling the wrong thing." [NAV-sell]

His advice to anyone in sales selling something they don't care about is to go find something else to sell
[NAV-sell]. He applies it to fundraising: he waits until his own excitement about the business crosses a threshold,
then explains what he sees, without exaggerating [NAV-sell]. Chapter 16 has the rest.

**A small craft point from 2010.** Drop filler words such as "uh" and "like", and learn to pause instead; he calls
it probably the best single thing you can do to sound more persuasive [VH-presentation-hacks].

## Deals: walk away early, fight rarely

> "You have to be able to walk away from deals." [NAV-sell]

His reasoning, our summary of [NAV-sell]:

- **Deals are long-term constraints.** A contract is two parties limiting their future options to work together
  on something bigger, like a stag hunt. Bad investors, bad contracts and bad partners are hard to exit.
- **Optionality is valuable.** "So I've just learned over the years that compromise is the enemy of building a
  great business." [NAV-sell]
- **Don't split small pies.** With nonlinear returns, the upside matters far more than the split today; he says
  people spend too much time on small stakes [NAV-sell].
- **But defend yourself when it matters.** If being taken advantage of would cost you sleep for a year, fight; if
  you can walk away and not work with them again, do [NAV-sell].

From 2019, when you're stuck in a one-off negotiation, turn it into a repeated game, and remember that
negotiations are won by whoever cares less [NAV-short]. From 2015, the deals he regrets are advisory roles that
looked like free stock and cost him time [TF-097].

## How to apply it (our reading)

**Before a sale or pitch:**
1. Write what you genuinely believe the product does for this buyer, and what it doesn't.
2. Say the "doesn't" out loud. That's the credibility [NAV-sell].
3. If you can't get excited, fix the product or pick a different thing to sell.

**Before signing a deal:**
1. How long does it lock you in, and how hard is it to exit [NAV-sell]?
2. What options does it close?
3. Is the upside 10x or more on your time or money? If not, why constrain yourself?
4. What's your walk-away, written down before the meeting?

**Worked scenario (invented).** A five-person software company is offered a reseller deal: 30% of its revenue for
three years, exclusive in its home region, with a two-year notice period. The reseller would roughly double sales
in year one. Lock-in: three years plus notice. Options closed: direct sales in the region. Upside: about 2x, not
10x. Walk-away: no exclusivity. Our reading: counter with a one-year non-exclusive term; if refused, walk away and
stay friendly, since the game may repeat [NAV-short].

**Failure modes (our reading):**
- Treating "walk away" as a tactic and bluffing. He means it as a real option.
- Overselling to win this quarter and losing credibility for the next decade.
- Fighting over small splits while a bigger opportunity waits.
- Accepting advisory shares you don't have time for [TF-097].

**Limits:** he admits he isn't the person for hitting a monthly sales quota [NAV-sell]. High-volume sales roles
need process and training this chapter doesn't cover.
