# 00 · Who Naval Ravikant is, for this playbook

This playbook is about one thing: how Naval Ravikant says wealth gets built, and the startup, investing,
learning, decision and happiness ideas that support building it. It doesn't cover his politics, his views on
crypto as an asset or a cause, his science and philosophy podcasts, or his personal life.

Citation IDs are in `SOURCES.md`: `[TWEET18 #n]` a tweet in the 2018 thread, linked · `[X yyyy-mm-dd]` a
standalone post on X, linked · `[RICH-01]` to
`[RICH-37]` the 2019 How to Get Rich podcast · `[NAV-slug]` a nav.al post or episode · `[ANGEL-1]` `[ANGEL-2]`
How to Angel Invest (2019, 2020) · `[TF-...]` Tim Ferriss Show transcripts · `[TKP-18]` The Knowledge Project
transcript (2017) · `[VH-...]` Venture Hacks · `[ALM]` the Almanack, compiled by Eric Jorgenson.

## What the sources cover

- **The 2018 tweetstorm.** *How to Get Rich (without getting lucky)* went out as a 40-tweet thread on 31 May
  2018 [TWEET18 #1](https://x.com/naval/status/1002103360646823936). Every tweet has its own link in
  `SOURCES.md`. To keep quoting from it short, this edition quotes only six of the tweets and paraphrases the
  rest, with a link to each.
- **Standalone posts.** 48 of his other posts on X, from 2010 to 2026, back or extend the chapters. Several are
  the first place a line appears, such as *Productize yourself* in 2010 [X 2010-09-29](https://x.com/naval/status/25854742061).
- **The 2019 podcast.** He and Babak Nivi went through the thread in 37 episodes on nav.al, from February to May
  2019 [RICH-01 to RICH-37], then answered listener questions (June 2019 to March 2020).
- **Earlier interviews.** The Tim Ferriss Show #97 (2015) and its Q&A follow-up (2016), and The Knowledge
  Project #18 (2017), show the same ideas before the thread [TF-097, TF-QA, TKP-18].
- **The 2025 and 2026 episodes.** Short episodes on learning by doing, agency and specific knowledge (July to
  October 2025), then longer ones on recruiting [NAV-curate-people], selling and deals [NAV-sell], and building
  with AI [NAV-code, NAV-industrial].
- **The framework is older than the thread.** His 2008 blog post already lists income as accountability plus
  leverage plus specific knowledge [NAV-life-formulas-i]. In 2019: "I came up with this framework when I was
  really young. We're talking decades and decades." [RICH-14]
- **Companies.** His first startups included Epinions, a product review site [RICH-32]. On AngelList: "I started
  AngelList partially because I was cut out of some very big deals early on" [ANGEL-1]. Venture Hacks, the blog
  he wrote with Nivi, was "all about the game theory of venture capital and helping entrepreneurs raise money."
  [ANGEL-1] In 2025 he said he had recently started another company [NAV-do].
- **Investing.** He calls Twitter "my first major angel investment that worked out." [ANGEL-1] In 2009 he
  described himself as having "been involved with starting about seven companies, been an investor as an angel
  or a VC in well over 30 companies" [VH-angel].
- **Where he started.** He describes himself as having started as a poor kid in India [RICH-04].

## Read him with his own caveats

> "In this tweetstorm I put down the minimum-viable principles." [NAV-accountability]

> "Even in this podcast, you should examine everything. If something doesn't feel true to you, put it down."
> [RICH-34]

> "This is not math or science or equations, and we're always changing our opinions." [ANGEL-1]

He also flags that the message has a pro-Internet bias because it is delivered through the Internet
[NAV-accountability], and that the angel material is "not investment advice" [ANGEL-1].

Two more caveats from the sources themselves:
- nav.al says of its podcast pages: "These transcripts have been edited for clarity." [RICH-01, nav.al page note]
  They are close to his spoken words, not verbatim audio.
- On the podcasts, the interviewer is Babak Nivi. Lines marked "Nivi" are his, and this playbook labels them.

Chapter 18 collects the limits in one place: survivorship, timing, place, and where his advice is weakest.

## How to use this chapter (our suggestion)

1. **Pick your entry point.** If all your income stops when you stop, start at 05. If you're founding, start
   at 11 and 14. If you're stuck on a choice, 10.
2. **Read the caveat that fits you.** Not in tech or near a tech hub? Read 18 first.
3. **Mark one claim you disagree with.** Test it against your own record before you use it.

**Use it now:** `SKILL.md` runs a full session; `templates/02-leverage-audit.md` is the quickest single start.

**Checks to run:**
1. Is your work in technology or near a tech hub? If not, which ideas need translating, and which still apply as
   they are?
2. Which of his caveats matters most for you: edited transcripts, a pro-Internet bias, or not investment advice?
3. Which claim in this playbook do you most disagree with, and what evidence from your own work would settle it?
