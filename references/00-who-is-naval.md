# 00 · Who Naval Ravikant is, for this playbook

This playbook is about one thing: how Naval Ravikant says wealth gets built, and the startup, investing,
learning, decision and happiness ideas that support building it. It doesn't cover his politics, his views on
crypto as an asset or a cause, his science and philosophy podcasts, or his personal life.

Citation IDs are in `SOURCES.md`: `[TWEET18 #n]` a tweet in the 2018 thread, linked · `[RICH-01]` to
`[RICH-37]` the 2019 How to Get Rich podcast · `[NAV-slug]` a nav.al post or episode · `[ANGEL-1]` `[ANGEL-2]`
How to Angel Invest (2019, 2020) · `[TF-...]` Tim Ferriss Show transcripts · `[VH-...]` Venture Hacks ·
`[ALM]` the Almanack, compiled by Eric Jorgenson.

## What the sources cover

- **The 2018 tweetstorm.** "How to Get Rich (without getting lucky):" went out as a 40-tweet thread on
  31 May 2018 [TWEET18 #1](https://x.com/naval/status/1002103360646823936). Every tweet in it has its own link in
  `SOURCES.md`.
- **The 2019 podcast.** He and Babak Nivi went through the thread in 37 episodes on nav.al, from February to May
  2019 [RICH-01 to RICH-37], then answered questions about it (June to July 2019) [NAV-accountability to
  NAV-externalities].
- **The framework is older than the thread.** A 2008 blog post already reads "Income = Accountability +
  Leverage + Specific Knowledge" [NAV-life-formulas-i]. In 2019 he said: "I came up with this framework when I
  was really young. We're talking decades and decades." [RICH-14] In 2020 he put it at age 13: "that tweetstorm
  is a series of principles that I kind of wrote for myself in my head when I was 13" [TF-473].
- **Companies.** "One of my first startups was Epinions, an online product review site that was independent of
  Amazon." [RICH-32] On AngelList: "I started AngelList partially because I was cut out of some very big deals
  early on" [ANGEL-1]. On Venture Hacks, the blog he co-wrote with Nivi: "Back in the day, we did Venture Hacks,
  which was all about the game theory of venture capital and helping entrepreneurs raise money." [ANGEL-1]
  In 2025: "I recently started another company. It's a very difficult project." [NAV-do]
- **Investing.** On Twitter: "I got a chance to invest in Twitter. That was my first major angel investment that
  worked out." [ANGEL-1] Nivi introduced him in 2019 as "a prolific tech investor in companies like Twitter,
  Uber, and many more." [RICH-01, Nivi introducing him] In 2016 Tim Ferriss listed "His deals include Twitter,
  Uber, Yammer, Postmates, Wish, Thumbtack, and OpenDNS." [TF-QA, Tim Ferriss's introduction] In 2009 Naval
  described himself as having "been involved with starting about seven companies, been an investor as an angel
  or a VC in well over 30 companies" [VH-angel].
- **Teaching investing.** "This podcast open-sources what we teach at Spearhead, a fund we created that trains
  the next generation of angel investors." [ANGEL-1]
- **Where he started.** "I started as a poor kid in India, so if I can make it, anybody can, in that sense."
  [RICH-04]

## Read him with his own caveats

> "In this tweetstorm I put down the minimum-viable principles." [NAV-accountability]

> "Even in this podcast, you should examine everything. If something doesn't feel true to you, put it down."
> [RICH-34]

> "This is not math or science or equations, and we're always changing our opinions." [ANGEL-1]

> "Obviously, this message is being delivered through the Internet, so it's going to have a pro-Internet bias."
> [NAV-accountability]

> "This is not investment advice; but you may find this useful if you're already in the profession or in the
> hobby of angel investing." [ANGEL-1]

Two more caveats from the sources themselves:
- nav.al says of its podcast pages: "These transcripts have been edited for clarity." [RICH-01, nav.al page note]
  They are close to his spoken words, not verbatim audio.
- On the podcast, the interviewer is Babak Nivi. Lines marked "Nivi" are his, and this playbook labels them.

**The move:** take the principles, test them against your own situation, and keep only what survives.

**Use it now:** `SKILL.md` runs a full session; `templates/02-leverage-audit.md` is the quickest single start.

**Checks to run:**
1. Is your work in technology or near a tech hub? If not, which ideas need translating, and which still apply as
   they are?
2. Which of his caveats matters most for you: edited transcripts, a pro-Internet bias, or not investment advice?
3. Which claim in this playbook do you most disagree with? He suggests starting there [NAV-accountability].
