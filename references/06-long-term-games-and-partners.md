## Compound interest, everywhere

> "Pick an industry where you can play long term games with long term people."
> [TWEET18 #7](https://x.com/naval/status/1002103770518441989)

> "Play iterated games. All the returns in life, whether in wealth, relationships, or knowledge, come from
> compound interest." [TWEET18 #9](https://x.com/naval/status/1002103908947263488)

> "You have to be able to play a long-term game. And long-term games are good not just for compound interest,
> they're also good for trust." [RICH-09]

Nivi's summary, which Naval called "a brilliant formulation" [RICH-09]: "In a long-term game, it seems that
everybody is making each other rich. And in a short-term game, it seems like everybody is making themselves
rich." [RICH-09, Nivi]

> "In a longterm game, it's positive sum. We're all baking the pie together." [RICH-09]

## What compounding buys you

> "The friction goes down, you can do bigger, and bigger things together." [RICH-09]

> "If I'm doing a deal with someone I've worked with for 20 years and there is mutual trust, we don't have to
> read the legal contracts." [NAV-relationships]

> "The most under-recognized reason startups fail is because the founders fall apart." [NAV-relationships]

Nivi, in the same episode: "It's better to have a few deep compounding relationships than many shallow,
non-compounding relationships." [NAV-relationships, Nivi]

What it looked like for him by 2020: "I have deep relationships with a dozen people who I know, and I trust that I
can do business with, for the rest of my life." [TF-473]

**The cost of switching:** "every time you wander out of where you built your network, you're going to be
starting from scratch." [RICH-09]

## Pick partners on three things

> "Pick business partners with high intelligence, energy, and, above all, integrity."
> [TWEET18 #10](https://x.com/naval/status/1002103969781448704)

| Trait | Why, in his words [RICH-11] |
|---|---|
| Intelligence | "You need someone who is smart, or they'll head in the wrong direction." [RICH-11] |
| Energy | "You need someone high-energy because the world is full of smart, lazy people." [RICH-11] |
| Integrity | Without it, "what you have is you have a smart and hard working crook, who's eventually going to cheat you." [RICH-11] |

He credits the three-part test to Warren Buffett [ANGEL-2].

### Read signals, not claims

> "And signals is what they do, not what they say." [RICH-11]

> "We all know that socially if someone treats a waiter, or waitress in a restaurant really badly, then it's only
> a matter of time until they treat you badly." [RICH-11]

> "Generally, the more someone is saying that they're moral, ethical, and high integrity, the less likely they
> are to be that way." [RICH-11]

> "So, you want to find long-term people. You want to find people who seem irrationally ethical." [RICH-11]

His test for himself, a line he says he read elsewhere and modified: "self-esteem is the reputation that you
have with yourself." [RICH-11]

### And energy for this specific thing

> "I almost won't start a company, or hire a person, or work with somebody if I just don't think they're into
> what I want them to do." [RICH-11]

### No cynics

> "Don't partner with cynics and pessimists. Their beliefs are self-fulfilling."
> [TWEET18 #11](https://x.com/naval/status/1002104083694501890)

> "Essentially, to create things, you have to be a rational optimist." [RICH-12]

> "And all the really successful people I know have a very strong action bias." [RICH-12]

From 2016: "If you see someone who's always fighting with somebody else, they're eventually going to fight with
you." [TF-QA]

## The one-day rule

What he called in 2016 his most popular tweet: "If you can't see yourself working with somebody for life, don't
work with them for a day." [TF-QA] How he uses it: "As soon as I know I'm not going to be working with someone 10
years from now, then I have to start extricating myself from that relationship or investing less effort in it."
[RICH-34]

## Ethics as long-term greed

> "Acting ethically turns out to be a selfish imperative." [NAV-ethics]

> "If you cut people fair deals, you won't get paid in the short term. But over the long term, everybody will
> want to deal with you." [NAV-ethics]

> "The longer your time horizon, the wiser you're going to seem to everybody around you." [NAV-ethics]

The fastest way to end the game: "The number one way people get ruined in modern business is not by betting too
much; it's by cutting corners and doing unethical or downright illegal things." [NAV-kelly-criterion]

## When you're stuck in a one-off game

> "If someone is taking advantage of you in a negotiation, your best option is to turn it from a short-term game
> into a long-term game." [NAV-short]

His ways to do it, with a house renovation as the example [NAV-short]: split the work into two projects and
decide on the second after the first; mention friends who are waiting to see how it goes; leave a public review.
And his rule for negotiations: "Negotiations are won by whoever cares less." [NAV-short]
