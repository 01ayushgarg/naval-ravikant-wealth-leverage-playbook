# 01 · Wealth, not money or status

## Three different things

> "Seek wealth, not money or status. Wealth is having assets that earn while you sleep."
> [TWEET18 #2](https://x.com/naval/status/1002103497725173760)

The same tweet goes on to define money as how we transfer time and wealth, and status as your place in a social
hierarchy [TWEET18 #2](https://x.com/naval/status/1002103497725173760). On the podcast he makes the contrast
concrete [RICH-01]:

| | His picture | Game |
|---|---|---|
| **Wealth** | "Wealth is the factory, the robots, that's cranking out things." [RICH-01] | Positive sum: things get made |
| **Money** | Social credits: a record of claims on other people's time [RICH-01] | A record of value already created |
| **Status** | "Status, on the other hand, is a zero-sum game." [RICH-01] | To win, someone else has to lose [RICH-01] |

A 2020 post sharpens the contrast: "Status games are multiplayer, zero-sum, hierarchical, judged socially."
[X 2020-05-31](https://x.com/naval/status/1267179384886095873) The games he favours are single player, positive
sum and judged by nature or markets, and they pay later in wealth, health and knowledge [X 2020-05-31](https://x.com/naval/status/1267179384886095873).

## What wealth is for

> "So, the purpose of wealth is freedom. It's nothing more than that." [RICH-01]

He is clear about its limits: money won't solve all your problems, but "it's going to solve all of your money
problems." [RICH-01] In 2020: "So to me, the purpose of money is freedom." [TF-473] By 2025 he put it more broadly:
"Wealth is the ability to make things happen." [X 2025-10-30](https://x.com/naval/status/1983776982145102307)

## Where it comes from

The thread's answer is to give society what it wants but doesn't yet know how to get, at scale
[TWEET18 #6](https://x.com/naval/status/1002103723848450049). On the podcast he adds that if society already knew
how to make it, it wouldn't need you, and that building one copy isn't enough: you have to scale it [RICH-08].
His recurring pattern, from chauffeurs to ride-hailing: "It's about distributing what rich people used to have
to everybody." [RICH-08]

He also insists it can be done ethically, and that secretly despising wealth makes it elude you
[TWEET18 #3](https://x.com/naval/status/1002103559276478464). What he means by wealth creation is making
things, "not about taking money." [RICH-02]

## Not luck: become the kind of person who makes money

> "A lot of people think making money is about luck. It's not. It's about becoming the kind of person that
> makes money." [RICH-04]

His test: "In 1,000 parallel universes, you want to be wealthy in 999 of them." [RICH-04] He lists four kinds of
luck, crediting the list to a book via a Marc Andreessen blog post [RICH-04]. Our summary:

| Kind (his names) | What it means, our paraphrase | What you control |
|---|---|---|
| Blind luck | Something outside your control happens [RICH-04] | Nothing |
| Luck from hustling | Enough motion and attempts that luck finds you [RICH-04] | Volume of attempts |
| Luck from preparation | You're skilled enough to spot a lucky break [RICH-04] | Skill in a field |
| Luck from your character | A unique character and brand that brings luck to you [RICH-04] | Reputation |

He calls the fourth a matter of character becoming destiny [RICH-05], and says his own wealth stacked up in small
pieces rather than one big year [RICH-04].

## Protect the freedom you're buying

> "people who are living far below their means enjoy a freedom that people busy upgrading their lifestyles just
> can't fathom." [RICH-07]

His advice is to make money in discrete lumps, spaced out, so your lifestyle doesn't adapt to it [RICH-07].

## No shortcuts, and check who's selling them

The thread says there are no get-rich-quick schemes, only someone else getting rich off you
[TWEET18 #38](https://x.com/naval/status/1002109022420451328). And: "If anyone is giving advice on how to get
rich and they're also making money off of it, they should have made their money elsewhere." [RICH-36] His 2025 version: "Nobody who's actually good at making
money needs to sell you a course on it." [X 2025-02-01](https://x.com/naval/status/1885783497601892782)

## Go at it indirectly

His 2025 version: you don't chase money directly; build something valuable with leverage, accountability and
specific knowledge and "you're going to make money as a byproduct." [NAV-indirect]

## How to apply it (our reading)

1. **Sort your goals into three columns:** wealth (assets), money (income), status (rank). Be honest about the
   third.
2. **List what earns while you sleep.** Equity, code, content, an audience, IP, investments. Put a monthly number
   on each.
3. **Compute a freedom ratio** (our metric, not his): monthly income from those assets divided by monthly costs.
4. **Pick one asset to start or grow this quarter**, using chapters 02 to 07.
5. **Hold lifestyle flat** while the ratio rises [RICH-07].

**Worked number (invented).** Priya earns $9,000 a month in salary and spends $6,000. Her assets: $40,000 in
index funds (about $130 a month at a 4% withdrawal rate) and nothing else. Freedom ratio: 130 ÷ 6,000 = 2%. A
side product earning $900 a month would take it to 17%; a $1,000 raise spent on a bigger flat would leave it at 2%
and raise costs. The ratio, not the salary, is the wealth number.

**Failure modes (our reading):**
- Counting salary as wealth. It stops when you stop.
- Chasing status assets (a title, a car) and calling them wealth.
- Buying a "how to get rich" course from someone whose wealth comes from selling the course [RICH-36].
- Lifestyle creep that cancels every gain [RICH-07].

**Limits:** the idea of assets that earn while you sleep assumes markets and property rights you can rely on, and some
starting savings. See chapter 18.

**Use it now:** section 1 of `templates/02-leverage-audit.md` lists what you own that earns while you sleep.

**Checks to run:**
1. List everything you own that earns while you sleep. If the list is empty, that's the finding.
2. Which of your goals this year are really status goals? What would you drop if nobody could see the result?
3. What does society want from you that it can't yet get, and could you deliver it a thousand times?
4. Has your lifestyle grown faster than your assets in the last two years?
