# 08 · Time and the hourly rate

Sources in this chapter: NAV-the-80-hour-myth (29 Nov 2005) · NAV-who-has-time-for-meetings (10 Apr 2010) ·
TF-QA (2 May 2016) · TWEET18 (31 May 2018) · RICH-27 to RICH-29 (1 to 8 May 2019) · TF-473 (15 Oct 2020).

## Set the rate, then enforce it

> "Set and enforce an aspirational personal hourly rate. If fixing a problem will save less than your hourly rate,
> ignore it. If outsourcing a task will cost less than your hourly rate, outsource it."
> [TWEET18 #35](https://x.com/naval/status/1002108599399661568)

> "No one is going to value you more than you value you. Set a high personal hourly rate, and stick to it."
> [RICH-27]

> "Fast-forward to your wealthy self and pick an intermediate hourly rate." [RICH-27]

His number: "Before I had any real money and you could hire me, I set an aspirational rate of $5,000 an hour."
[RICH-27] How high is high enough: "It should seem and feel absurdly high. If it doesn't, it's not high enough."
[RICH-27]

### The arithmetic

> "Factor your time into every decision." [RICH-27]

> "Say you value your time at $100 an hour. If you decide to spend an hour driving across town to get something,
> you're effectively throwing away $100." [RICH-27]

**The move** (our arithmetic, not his): for any task, compare hours × your aspirational rate with what it would
cost to outsource it, or with what it saves. If the task is worth less than your hours, outsource it, ignore it,
or drop it.

### He didn't always follow it

> "Of course, I still ended up doing stupid things like arguing with the electrician or returning the broken
> speaker. But I shouldn't have." [RICH-27]

### Penny-pinching is a different goal

> "You can penny-pinch your way to basic sustenance." [RICH-27] "If you're going to create wealth, it has to be
> your number-one, overwhelming priority." [RICH-27]

## Work hard, on the right thing, with the right people

> "Work as hard as you can. Even though who you work with and what you work on are more important than how hard
> you work." [TWEET18 #36](https://x.com/naval/status/1002108800457883650)

> "But hard work is no substitute for who you work with and what you work on." [RICH-28]

His order [RICH-28]: pick the area, then the people, then work as hard as you can. He calls them "like three legs
of a stool" [RICH-28].

### Sprints, not marathons

> "Nobody really works 80 to 120 hours a week at high output, with mental clarity." [RICH-28]

> "It's more like a lion hunting and less like a marathoner running." [RICH-28]

The same idea in 2005: "In fact, your best work was probably done in tremendous, focused bursts, surrounded by
long periods of dullness and inactivity." [NAV-the-80-hour-myth] "First, measure outputs, not inputs, in
yourself and your organization." [NAV-the-80-hour-myth]

> "When you have inspiration, act on it right then and there." [RICH-28]

> "Impatience with actions, patience with results." [RICH-28]

## Make money with your mind, not your time

> "The ideal would be to make money with your mind, not with your time." [TF-473]

> "So if I can just make one good decision a year, and that makes me all the money that I need for that year,
> then that's perfect." [TF-473]

But first the reps: "You have to put in all the iterations, 10,000 iterations, not 10,000 hours, to figure out
what to do and how to do it well." [TF-473]

## Guard the calendar

> "You should be too busy to "do coffee," while still keeping an uncluttered calendar."
> [TWEET18 #34](https://x.com/naval/status/1002108466809323521)

> "The only way to do that is to constantly, and ruthlessly, decline meetings." [RICH-29]

His escalation ladder [RICH-29]: if they want a meeting, offer a call; if a call, offer email; if email, offer a
text. Keep meetings short, small and walking. "Nothing is getting done in a meeting with eight people around a
conference table." [RICH-29]

When it's fine to say yes: "That's fine early in your career, when you're still exploring." [RICH-29]

If you're the one asking: "Product progress is the entrepreneur's resume. It's an unfake-able resume."
[RICH-29] And from 2010: "Written always beats a video or screencast, since most intelligent people can read a
lot faster than they can listen." [NAV-who-has-time-for-meetings]

> "A busy calendar and a busy mind will destroy your ability to do great things in this world." [RICH-29]

From 2016:

> "Busy is a death of productivity and happiness." [TF-QA]

> "I also encourage taking at least one day a week preferably two, because if you budget two, you'll end up with
> one." [TF-QA]

> "It's only after you're bored that you have the great ideas." [TF-QA]

> "Guard your time. It's all you have." [TF-QA]
