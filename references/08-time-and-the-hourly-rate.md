## Set the rate, then enforce it

The thread's rule: set and enforce an aspirational personal hourly rate; if fixing a problem saves less than that
rate, ignore it, and if outsourcing a task costs less, outsource it
[TWEET18 #35](https://x.com/naval/status/1002108599399661568).

> "Fast-forward to your wealthy self and pick an intermediate hourly rate." [RICH-27]

His own number, before he had real money, was $5,000 an hour [RICH-27]. How high is high enough: "It should seem
and feel absurdly high. If it doesn't, it's not high enough." [RICH-27] He admits he still argued with the
electrician and returned the broken speaker, and says he shouldn't have [RICH-27]. When he reposted the rule in
2019 he added the social cost: "Get comfortable disappointing people whose expectations will eat your life up, one
hour at a time." [X 2019-05-20](https://x.com/naval/status/1130530315607396358)

## Work hard, on the right thing, with the right people

The thread says to work as hard as you can, while noting that who you work with and what you work on matter more
([#36](https://x.com/naval/status/1002108800457883650)). His order on the podcast: pick the area, then the
people, then work as hard as you can, "like three legs of a stool" [RICH-28].

**Sprints, not marathons.** "It's more like a lion hunting and less like a marathoner running." [RICH-28] The
2005 version: "In fact, your best work was probably done in tremendous, focused bursts, surrounded by long
periods of dullness and inactivity." [NAV-the-80-hour-myth] And: "Impatience with actions, patience with
results." [RICH-28]
A 2017 post gives the model: "Knowledge workers function like athletes - train and sprint, then rest and
reassess." [X 2017-06-10](https://x.com/naval/status/873624849230385152)

## Make money with your mind, not your time

> "So if I can just make one good decision a year, and that makes me all the money that I need for that year,
> then that's perfect." [TF-473]

But first the reps: "10,000 iterations, not 10,000 hours" [TF-473].

## Guard the calendar

The thread says to be too busy to "do coffee" while keeping an uncluttered calendar
([#34](https://x.com/naval/status/1002108466809323521)). On the podcast he says the only way is to decline meetings
constantly and ruthlessly [RICH-29]. His ladder [RICH-29]: if they want a meeting, offer a call; if a call, email;
if email, a text. Saying yes is fine early in your career, while you're exploring [RICH-29].

If you're the one asking: "Product progress is the entrepreneur's resume. It's an unfake-able resume." [RICH-29]
From 2010: written beats video, because most smart people read faster than they listen
[NAV-who-has-time-for-meetings].

From 2016: "Busy is a death of productivity and happiness." [TF-QA] He suggests budgeting two free days a week to
end up with one [TF-QA]. From 2015, on investing: "Guard your time more carefully than you guard your money."
[TF-097]

## How to apply it (our reading)

1. **Compute your effective rate:** last month's income ÷ all hours worked, including admin.
2. **Set an aspirational rate** 5 to 10 times higher [RICH-27]. It should feel absurd.
3. **Audit one week.** For each task: hours × aspirational rate against the cost to outsource or the value
   saved.
4. **Cut three tasks** that fall under the rate: outsource, automate or drop.
5. **Protect one empty day** a week and use it for the sprint that matters.

**Worked number (invented).** Tomás earns $7,200 a month over 180 hours: $40 an hour effective. He sets $300 as
his aspirational rate. Grocery runs (4 hours a month) cost $1,200 at $300 against $60 for delivery: outsource.
Hand-reconciling invoices (6 hours) costs $1,800 against $49 a month for software: automate. A weekly status
meeting (4 hours) produces no decisions: replace with a written update. He frees 14 hours a month and puts them
into the side product that could earn without him.

**Failure modes (our reading):**
- Setting the rate, then never using it to say no.
- Outsourcing what builds your specific knowledge. Keep the steep-learning-curve work [NAV-finding-time].
- Treating busyness as output [NAV-the-80-hour-myth].
- Spending the freed hours on more rented time instead of ownership.

**Limits:** early in a career, or with no savings, some low-rate work is the price of learning and of paying rent.
He himself says meetings are fine while you're exploring [RICH-29].
